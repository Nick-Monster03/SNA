DECISIONS OF PHASES 2 AND 4 (DATA, NETWORKS AND HYPOTHESIS TESTS) AND WHY
Erasmus+ project - Network Analysis - updated on 18/11/2026

SOURCE
Dataset by Vaisanen et al. (2025, Scientific Data), version 1.1 on Zenodo
(file Erasmus_2014-2023_individual.parquet). The year in the dataset is the year in which the
mobility starts.
Limit: the paper validates the data up to 2022 (accuracy above 96%); 2023 goes beyond that
validation.

DATA AND FILTERS
1. Only "for studies" mobility (78% of the participants 2016-2023).
   Why: the question is about students who go to study at a university abroad. Excluded:
   - traineeships, because the destination is a company and not a university;
   - vocational training (VET) and youth activities (Youth), because they involve people who
     are not university students.
   Study mobility with partner countries is also included (about 9,000 students), because it
   has a NUTS region both at departure and at arrival.
2. Years 2016-2023.
   Why:
   - 2014-2015 are excluded because they use the NUTS 2013 codes;
   - the GDP file starts in 2016;
   - 2023 is needed to have two post-Covid years (2022-23) and to see the effect of Brexit
     (departures from the United Kingdom from 5,349 in 2022 to 705 in 2023).
3. Switzerland excluded (963 students, 0.06%).
   Why: it does not take part in the programme. In the data it never sends students and only
   receives them from 2021. The rule is general: only the countries that send students in
   every year are kept.
4. Flows within the same country removed (1,273 students).
   Why: Erasmus is mobility abroad.
5. NUTS codes changed in 2021: regions are merged following the cities that change code:
   - Continental Croatia = HR04;
   - Southern and Western Norway = NO01+NO03+NO04+NO05 (Oslo og Akershus, Sor-Ostlandet,
     Agder og Rogaland, Vestlandet; NO02, NO06, NO07 stay separate);
   - Northern Ireland = UKN0.
   ES64 and NO0B removed (18 students), with no correspondence.
   Why: every region must be the same territory in every year.

NETWORKS
6. Nodes = 319 NUTS 2 regions of 36 countries; directed edges origin -> destination;
   weight = students.
   Why: NUTS 2 is the level of the Eurostat GDP; countries are too few, NUTS 3 regions too
   small.
7. Three period networks: pre_covid 2018-19, covid 2020-21, post_covid 2022-23.
   The weight is the yearly average. The yearly networks 2016-2023 are used for time series.
   Why: the same design on three networks; two years are more stable than one.
8. Binary network: edge if it has at least 5 students per year; check with threshold 3.
   Why: with threshold 5, 80% of the students and over 92% of the regions stay connected.
   Threshold 10 would be a stricter check (to do if time allows), but with threshold 10 the
   Covid network breaks apart (only 82% of the regions stay connected).
9. Small-world on the UNDIRECTED binary version: there is an edge if there is at least one
   direction.
   Why: the clustering and average path length of the small-world are defined on undirected
   networks. One direction is enough because a stable flow in one direction is already a tie
   between the two regions; requiring reciprocity would delete many ties (Erasmus is often
   unbalanced).
10. The United Kingdom stays in the networks.
    Why: its fall (from 11.5% of the flows in 2018 to 2.9% in 2023) is part of H3.
    For H3 the United Kingdom is looked at year by year (yearly networks), because the
    2022-23 average hides the collapse of 2023 (departures from 5,349 in 2022 to 705 in 2023).

ATTRIBUTES AND HYPOTHESES
11. GDP per capita in euro (Eurostat), average of the two years of each period.
    Merged regions are recombined with the population-weighted average.
    Why: it measures wealth without depending on the size of the region.
    Limit: it is in current euros, with no correction for inflation. It is fine for comparing
    regions within the same period, but not for comparing the periods with each other.
12. GDP is missing for the United Kingdom, Iceland and Liechtenstein (43 regions): they stay in
    the networks and in the distances, but leave the GDP analyses, with a note in the report.
    Why: they are not in the Eurostat file used. Mixing different sources or estimating the
    values would complicate the analysis and make the data less comparable.
13. GDP approximations, to be written in the Data section:
    - PT16 Centro and PT18 Alentejo are approximated, because in 2024 Eurostat redrew them and
      there is no exact correspondence;
    - for Norway, post_covid uses the 2021 GDP, the last one available in the file;
    - NL31 Utrecht and NL33 Zuid-Holland have the new codes (NL35 and NL36) in the GDP file:
      their value is used, recoded, with no other change;
    - for Albania (AL01, AL02, AL03) post_covid has the GDP of only one of the two years: that
      year is used.
14. H2: the same sample (the 276 regions with GDP) in all models.
    Why: otherwise the models are not comparable.
15. H2: pairs of the same country (column same_country in the file region_pairs.csv) are
    removed.
    Why: their flow is zero by construction (point 4), not because of distance.
16. Distance: great-circle distance between the centres of the regions.
    Language: "same language" = a shared national official language; "same family" = the same
    language branch (Romance, Germanic, Slavic...).
    Choice: Croatian, Serbian and Montenegrin are different languages (not "same language"),
    but they are in the same family.
    Why: they are the distinct official languages of the three states.
    Changed in Phase 4 (point 22): the H2 tests use the most spoken language; this coding is
    kept as a check.

HYPOTHESIS TESTS (PHASE 4)
Scripts: 05_analysis/05_h1_wealth.py, 06_h2_mrqap.py, 07_h3_change.py. Table with the outcome
of the tests: 06_report/table_hypothesis_tests.md.
17. All p-values come from permutations (seed 42, as in Phase 3).
    Why: the regions of a network are not independent observations, so the classic p-values
    are not valid. Example: in H2 (M1, pre_covid) the classic regression would give for
    distance t = -7.9 ("highly significant"), the permutation test p = 0.37.
18. H1: dependent variable = net balance (in - out)/(in + out) of the weighted network;
    explanatory variable = log GDP per capita of the period; 5,000 permutations.
    Three models: all regions with GDP; without the 26 Turkish regions; within each country
    (differences from the country mean, permutations only among regions of the same country).
    Why:
    - the net balance does not depend on the size of the region;
    - with the log, inflation only shifts the intercept: the slopes of the three periods can be
      compared even though GDP is in current euros (point 11);
    - the Turkish regions (poor and strong senders, net balance about -0.9) could create the
      effect on their own;
    - the within-country model removes everything that is the same in the whole country
      (language, average cost of living, image of the country).
    In post_covid NL13 Drenthe has no incoming or outgoing students: it leaves the test.
    Limit: the prestige of single universities cannot be controlled for.
19. H2: MRQAP with Double Semi-Partialling (Dekker, Krackhardt and Snijders 2007, the method of
    netlm in R), 1,000 permutations. Dependent variable = log(1 + students per year); check
    with the binary network (1 = edge with at least 5 students per year, linear probability
    model).
    Why: about 60% of the pairs have zero flow; the log keeps the zeros and reduces the weight
    of the few very large flows. The binary version does not depend on the size of the flows.
    The method is written in Python (numpy), because R is not used in the project; the
    coefficients were checked against a full regression.
20. H2: three models.
    - M1: as in the roadmap (distance, same language, same family, GDP difference);
    - M2: M1 + size of origin and destination = students sent to and received from all
      regions, United Kingdom included;
    - M3: M1 + one fixed effect for every origin region and one for every destination region.
      Added after M1 and M2, as a check.
    Why:
    - without size, distance can be confused with how large the regions are;
    - size comes from the students and not from total GDP: src/04 does not need to change and
      it is a more direct measure of the university size of a region;
    - the fixed effects absorb everything that characterises a region, measured or not (size,
      prestige, climate, cost of living). They answer the objection "distance seems not to
      matter because the distant destinations in the South are more attractive".
    The conclusions are the same in the three models.
21. H2: subject similarity (column Field of Education of the dataset) does not enter the
    models.
    Why: the data exist (99.97% of the students have an ISCED field), but the similarity between
    two regions depends mostly on their size (correlation 0.39-0.45: large regions have all the
    subjects and "look like" everybody). With size in the model, the distance coefficient does
    not change; the fixed effects of M3 already cover the characteristics of every region.
    Limit: the result of Gadar et al. on subjects concerns institutions and cannot be seen
    with a regional network.
22. Language in H2 (changes point 16): "same language" = the most spoken language is the same
    in the two regions; "same family" = the two most spoken languages are in the same family.
    Every region has one language only: the first language of its country in languages.csv
    (for the countries with several official languages - BE, CY, FI, IE, LU, MT - the first one
    is the most spoken), except:
    - Montenegro (ME00): Serbian (2023 census, mother tongue: Serbian 43.2%, Montenegrin 34.5%);
    - Brussels (BE10) and Wallonia (BE31-BE35): French; Flanders stays Dutch;
    - Aland (FI20): Swedish;
    - Bolzano/Bozen (ITH1): German.
    Why: with point 16 many pairs were "same language" only because of small minorities
    (Belgium-Germany for the German-speaking community, Finland-Sweden, Cyprus-Turkey). With
    the new coding the ordered pairs with the same language fall from 2,886 to 1,260:
    Austria-Germany, Belgium-France, Belgium-Netherlands, South Tyrol-Austria and Germany,
    Cyprus-Greece, Aland-Sweden, Montenegro-Serbia.
    Note: point 16 kept Montenegrin and Serbian separate; with the most spoken language
    Montenegro and Serbia have the same language (8 pairs).
    The coding of point 16 is kept as a check (table h2_language_check.csv).
    Result: with the most spoken language the same-language effect stays negative but is
    smaller (M1 from -24% to -33%; M2 and M3 from -5% to -15%); it is not significant only in
    post_covid with M2 (p = 0.06). With the coding of point 16 it would be stronger (from -17%
    to -36%).
    To write in the report: the choice of the coding and the check with the coding of point 16.
23. H3 is mainly a descriptive comparison between networks (roadmap, 7.3). The only formal test
    is the QAP correlation between the period matrices: all 319 regions, cross-country pairs
    only, 1,000 permutations, with two schemes (free permutation; permutation only among
    regions of the same country).
    Why: the second scheme tells how much of the stability belongs to the single regions and
    not only to the countries.
    In the report: "consistent with a Covid effect", not a proof of cause.
24. H3: "normal" years = 2016-2019. The year-to-year changes of these years are the reference
    for judging the 2019-2020 jump (correlation, edges kept, density, reciprocity, Gini, share
    of the top 10%, k_max).
    Why: it tells whether the 2020 change is outside the normal variation of the network.
    Limit: three changes give a range, not a distribution: no p-value.
25. H3, Brexit: comparison 2019-2023 (last normal year before Covid and first year after the
    end of the United Kingdom's projects) and a year-by-year series with the index 2019 = 100,
    United Kingdom against the rest of the network.
    Why:
    - Covid lowers everybody, Brexit only the United Kingdom: the comparison with the rest of
      the network separates the two effects;
    - 2022 is already partly "after Brexit" (UK departures at 60 against 93 for the rest of the
      network);
    - a difference-in-differences is not used: the United Kingdom was already losing weight
      before 2019 (arrivals from 114 to 100 between 2016 and 2019, in the rest of the network
      from 94 to 100). This must be written in the report.
    Limit: 2023 goes beyond the validation of the dataset (SOURCE); but the rest of the network
    in 2023 is at 99-100, so the UK collapse does not depend on the data quality of that year.
26. Factors that can confound H3, to be cited among the limits (they cannot be measured with
    these data): the new Erasmus+ programme 2021-2027 and the projects of the old programme
    extended because of Covid, which overlap in 2021-2023.
